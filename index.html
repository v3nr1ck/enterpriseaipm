Buy every PM a license. Run a training. You get the same documents, faster.

A working prototype is now cheaper than the meeting about the prototype. Most programs ignore that and accelerate the paperwork.

A PRD is an opinion with formatting.

Documents are proxies

Building the real thing used to be expensive, so organizations argued about descriptions of it. A document cannot be tested. It can only be believed. Belief takes meetings.

The cost flipped

A PM can now answer a feasibility debate with a deployed prototype in less time than it takes to get the debate on a calendar. The proxy is now the expensive object.

The usual buy

Licenses and a training. Declare victory. The org needed fewer proxies. It bought a machine that writes them faster.

What to change

The system the PMs sit in. A bar you can see. Gates a launch can fail. A commons so one person’s tool is not a personal hobby. A number that could go the wrong way.

The ladder

Five levels. Judge them by what this person’s work changes for other people. Not by how clever the prompts look.

1

Assisted

A faster keyboard.

Drafting and summarizing on the same workflow. This is where most trainings stop.

2

Fluent

They pick the model on purpose.

Context is built, not pasted. One person gets faster. Peers start asking how.

3

Builder

The answer is a thing that runs.

Prototypes, pipelines, dashboards. Engineering can evaluate the artifact instead of interpreting a slide.

4

Orchestrator

Other people install what they made.

Tools that leave their laptop. This is the first level where the gain is not personal.

5

Multiplier

The job itself changed.

The ladder, the gates, and the numbers are how the org works. It still works if they quit.

Most orgs: many 1s, a few 3s, no 5s. Four and five are not skills. They are permissions. Someone with authority has to let the PM job be defined differently.

Seven gates

Questions a launch has to answer. If a team cannot fail the test, it is not a test.

1

Grounding

Every answer cites its source, or it does not ship to people whose decisions cost money.

2

Data boundary

Which data reaches which model, under what agreement. “It’s just a prompt” is how data leaves the building.

3

Accountability

A named human owns every output that reaches a customer. No name, no ship.

4

Failure design

Price the wrong answer before the incident. Afterward you are just writing the press release.

5

Accessibility

WCAG 2.2 AA in the first release. Excluded users do not file bugs. They leave.

6

Measurement

A baseline, and a metric that could show you failed. Enthusiasm is not a number.

7

Disclosure

People know when a machine wrote it. They find out anyway. The only choice is whether you said so.

A principle describes intent. A gate can stop a launch.

Ninety days

Baseline first. Then the commons. Then promotions. Most programs reverse that and stay a deck.

0-30

Measure, then pick the seed

  • Time the real cycles: discovery to PRD, PRD to decision, question to answer. Someone will ask what improved. You will need the before.
  • Map people onto the ladder. Expect a pyramid.
  • The 3s already in the building are the seed. Give them a sanctioned sandbox so this stops being shadow IT.

31-60

Build the commons

  • A place where a PM-built agent or template is installable by anyone. One 4 becomes a hundred faster 1s.
  • Put the seven gates in the intake and the PRD. A form field, not a memo.
  • Publish the first before-and-after numbers. Volunteers show up for evidence. Mandates get compliance.

61-90

Put it in promotions

  • The ladder goes into the career framework. That is the only piece that survives a new VP.
  • A small board for gate exceptions. An owner and a queue. Not a vibe.
  • Rotate the seed into each product family as named practice leads.
